## Rebuild
- **Second-stage channelizer for narrow modes not built** (2026-10-23): every channel is cut from the source's 65536-point FFT; `ChannelSpec::parent` is carried but refused (`BusError::NestedChannelsNotSupported`). Build it with the first narrow mode that needs it.
- **Channel output rates are `fs/2^k`** (2026-10-23): the channelizer's output rate is `fs·K/N` with K a power of two, so a consumer gets the smallest such rate at or above what it asked for, not an exact rate. Add a per-channel rational resampler if a consumer needs one.
- **No channel sharing between subscriptions** (2026-10-23): each subscription computes its own channel, even when two consumers want the same one. Add sharing if profiling shows it matters.
- **Channel samples carry the filter's group delay** (2026-10-23): a `Samples` delivery's content lags its `first_sample` by N/4 input samples (K/4 output samples); not corrected.
- **Sample waker declined** (2026-10-23): the stage polls the ring every 1 ms for its next block instead of being woken by the writer.
- **Ring hole-eviction stat and offer back-off** (done 2026-10-23, step 2): the ring counts the holes it evicts and backs off after refused offers (Task 1 of step 2), closing those step 1 leftovers.
- **Ring offers are not demoted with age** (2026-10-23): the keeper offers each block once, at `Normal`, as it leaves the guaranteed window, and does not re-offer it at a lower priority as it ages (spec §4.1 "priority falling with age"). Needs a reclaim-and-re-offer (or an OS call that changes priority in place) per band crossing; weigh its cost before step 5.
- **Ring user cap and per-source pin/weight UI not wired** (2026-10-23): the budget takes a ceiling in bytes and a per-source weight, but the user cap in seconds or bytes ("lower wins", spec §4.1) and the per-source pin/weight setting have no config or UI yet. Step 3 (UI) or later.
- **No memory-pressure handling on fallback platforms** (2026-10-23): outside Windows and Linux the ring's backend refuses every offer, and the spec's "poll available memory and retire the oldest blocks under a floor" is not built, so the ring there holds its whole ceiling whatever else needs RAM. Not scheduled (no such platform is targeted yet).
- **A Lost block in the middle of the ring is retired only at the front** (2026-10-23): reads of it fail with `Lost` at once, but the keeper drops it (and frees its slot) only once it is the oldest held block, so its memory is held until then. Step 5, if the replay scrubber makes it visible.
- **backend-airspy does not emit DeviceLost itself** (2026-10-23): spec §8 asks the Airspy backend to emit `DeviceLost`; instead the engine's watchdog raises it for any backend (no samples for 2 s and `is_streaming() == false`, see the `sdr-core` stream contract). An in-band event from libairspy's transfer status remains possible later.

## Detection

- **RDS on detected FM broadcast stations** (done 2026-10-11, checked on air: WIMZ 103.5 and WWST 102.1 name themselves on the chips with PS and full RadioText within 15 s; 105.5 is mono without RDS and 97.5 carries none; PS is published only once two assemblies agree): `detect::rds` (pilot-locked 57 kHz BPSK, block sync, burst correction, PI → call sign, PS, RT, PTY, AF, CT) and `engine::rds_monitor` (one station at a time per live source, revisit every 10 s) put the station on its occupant chip (`FM broadcast · KXYZ "PS"`). Left: RT/PTY in the inspector; HD Radio PSD once HD decode exists.
- **ATSC / ATSC 3.0 programme data.** ATSC 1.0 is 8-VSB in 6 MHz, so an R2 at 10 MS/s sees a whole channel; channel names and the programme guide live in the PSIP tables (VCT, EIT, ETT) of the 19.4 Mb/s transport stream, which means a full 8-VSB receiver (timing, equaliser, trellis + RS decoding) before any table can be read: feasible in software (GNU Radio has one) but CPU-heavy and a big piece of work. ATSC 3.0 is OFDM with LDPC; the bootstrap and L1 signalling are open and would at least identify the service, but service lists (SLT over ROUTE/IP) need most of a 3.0 receiver, and no mature open demodulator exists.
- **LTE / NR cell identification** (done 2026-10-11, checked on air: 739.0 → "LTE 10 MHz PCI 22 (4 ports)", 751.0 → PCI 303, 763.0 → PCI 70, SFN advancing by 20 per 0.2 s slice; all three local cells are 4-port, which the first cut could not decode; focus sources now take the survey's occupants as hints because a carrier filling the span has no floor to be found by): `detect::lte` does cyclic-prefix numerology (15/30 kHz), PSS/SSS (PCI, timing, CFO) and the MIB (bandwidth, SFN, 1 or 2 antenna ports, soft-combined over 4 frames); `engine::cell_monitor` checks each cellular occupant in a live span every 20 s (120 s once identified) and the chip reads e.g. "LTE 10 MHz PCI 217 (2 ports)". Left: NR PSS/SSS (numerology only today), 4-port MIB, thresholds on air, a per-market licensee table in `bandplan` for the operator; SIB1 not planned.
- **GSM detector** (done 2026-10-11, synthetic tests only; nothing on air here): `detect::gsm` finds the FCCH tone bursts (CFO), decodes the SCH (BSIC, frame number) and the classifier labels 150–300 kHz channels "GSM BCCH BSIC n-m".
- **Auto-squelch flashes on wideband signals** (done 2026-10-11, watch on air): a known occupant's allowed misses grow with its sightings (3 + up to 12 looks) and its extent is the union of its last four cuts.
- **HD Radio (NRSC-5) decode.** The hybrid digital sidebands are already detected in Focus; decoding them (OFDM sync, layer 1 FEC, HDC audio) is a large piece of work and the audio codec is proprietary, so start with station/programme metadata (SIS) and see how far the open descriptions go.

## Hardware

- **capture_channel drops samples at 5 MS/s cf32 output** (seen 2026-10-11: the 751 MHz LTE capture came out 32768 samples short with no warning; the example's ring follow-up falls behind). Warn on a ring overrun and lower the default output rate.

- **Manual Airspy gain stages** (done 2026-10-11): Gain → Manual gives LNA, mixer and VGA sliders (AGC off) through `GainSetting::Manual`; the hop profile is keyed by it. Untested on hardware tonight.
- **tinySA Ultra support** (on hand). A swept spectrum analyzer, not an SDR: no IQ, so no look-back or decoding. Three uses:
  - **Calibration reference:** reads in dBm, so measuring the same signal on both gives per-device dBFS→dBm offsets for the Airspys.
  - **Test source:** its signal-generator output gives known tones to measure Airspy gain tables and passband shape, and to test detection repeatably.
  - **Survey outside the R2's range:** about 100 kHz–6 GHz (HF below 24 MHz; 1.8–6 GHz for cellular, Wi-Fi, drone links).
  - Needs a second kind of source, a *power-sweep device* producing spectrum rows directly (USB serial text protocol). The Survey role and burst detector already work on rows. Check its COM port and firmware commands before writing it.
- **R2s hear each other's local oscillator** (partly done 2026-10-11): the survey blanks each focus Airspy's centre + 5 MHz ± 15 kHz as it retunes (`Source::set_masks`), besides hiding survey detections inside focus spans. Left: the weaker spur offsets (measure them), and the attenuation/filter suggestion.
- **Two R2s on one USB bus interfere.** With both R2s sharing a hub, the sweeping one put a dense comb of narrow carriers into the other (856.6–859.5 MHz). On separate buses it's gone. Worth a note in docs and maybe a warning when two Airspys share a hub.
- **A second Airspy sometimes can't be opened while the first streams** (`AIRSPY_ERROR_NOT_FOUND`), seen in the `classify_sim` example but not in the app. libairspy opens each device in turn to read serials; the exact trigger isn't known yet.
- **Recover when a device is unplugged** (done 2026-10-11, untested on hardware): a lost device is rescanned every 5 s and reopened into the same slot with its tuning when its serial is back; the pane title says DEVICE LOST meanwhile.
