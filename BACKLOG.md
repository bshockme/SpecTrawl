## UI

- **New-signal highlighting.** Draw emitters first seen recently (and signals not in the baseline) in a distinct colour/outline on the survey and focus waterfalls and at the top of the emitter table, fading back to normal after a while; pairs with the "new since baseline" filter below.
- **Time-based survey waterfall.** Rows are one per sweep pass now, so their spacing follows the sweep. Option to make each row a fixed time slice (e.g. 1 s): hops write into the cells of the current row as they arrive, and a slow or partial pass leaves cells unchanged (or dimmed) instead of stretching a row. Needs the row store to be writable in place, which also covers "fill the newest row hop by hop".
- **Survey waterfall at native resolution.** Render the survey history at the sweep's own bin and row resolution and scale that to the view, so zooming and panning are view-only and never change sweep parameters.
- **Keyboard shortcuts for nudging frequency coverage.** Step a device's centre (and the survey span edges) up and down by a configurable amount from the keyboard.
- **UI restructure** per `docs/ui-design.md`: all seven steps are in (receiver letters and selection, transport bar, inspector, receiver pane headers, egui_dock with saved/named workspaces, pop-out OS windows with their own docks, Trunking tab over `TrunkSystem`, Calls/Grants toggle, icon rail, Ctrl+K command palette with Space/M/1-2-3/Esc/Ctrl+Shift+P keys). A Diagnostics tab followed (per-receiver stats, sweep hop ages, audio queue, window/layout); the first run opens maximized with a layout built for the monitor, and the window geometry lives in the workspace. Left: `TrunkSystem` entries for DMR Cap+/Con+/Tier III, NXDN, LTR and EDACS as their decoders arrive. The new UI has only been smoke-tested on the simulator (screenshots), not clicked through on air.
- **Show the span click-to-tune will land on.** When hovering the survey (or the focus pane), draw the bracket the focus device would cover if clicked (centre ± usable bandwidth), not just a centre-frequency cursor.
- **Backfill the focus waterfall from survey history after a click-to-tune.** Copy the survey rows covering the new focus span into the focus waterfall so it doesn't start blank.
  - Survey rows are coarse (~54 kHz bins over 24–1800 MHz) and ~2.6 s apart. Stretch each one over the matching stretch of focus rows (≈130 rows at 50 rows/s) and resample the bins.
  - Draw the backfilled part dimmed or hatched so it doesn't pass for real focus data.

## Sweep / survey

- **Split or overlapping survey across several devices.** Let two or more SDRs act as survey devices: either each takes a slice of the span (pass time divides by the device count) or they sweep the same span out of phase (rows arrive twice as often). Needs the hop plan split per device, a merged row, and care about the R2s hearing each other's LO (Hardware).
- **Aviation scanner mode.** AM-only over the air bands (108–137 MHz, plus UHF military 225–400 MHz if wanted): 8.33/25 kHz channel grid, AM demod with carrier-based squelch (AM carrier is easy to squelch on, unlike the noise-floor approach used for FM), scan the detected carriers, hold on voice, label channels from a user list (tower, ground, approach) and show the last-active list.
- **Super-scanner mode.** Survey a set of ranges and have the focus devices tune themselves to signals found there, under adjustable rules: always the newest signal, the range with the most signals, stay on the current one until it ends, priority lists. Builds on segmented survey and the emitter table.
- **Segmented survey mode.** Sweep only user-specified ranges (several disjoint segments) instead of one contiguous span.
- **Auto-explore modes.** Designed in `docs/auto-explore.md` (2026-10-10): a per-receiver Explore mode that visits survey emitters from a shared, filtered queue, adaptive dwell to a verdict, an exploration log tab, in-place determination of emitters already in a focus span. Not started.
- **Survey sometimes drops to one hop per second after a retune.** Changing the survey's frequencies occasionally leaves the sweep crawling at ~1 hop/s until something kicks it; cause unknown (lock-flag wait, settle check, or a stale hop plan?). Reproduce with the Diagnostics tab's hop ages open.
- **Survey detection is dense.** Every persistent emitter (TV, LTE, paging) becomes a long record, so the survey shows many boxes (now drawn faintly). Needs a "new since baseline" view rather than boxes for everything.
  - Emitter table shows Focus by default. Next: show the survey as busy *bands* (one row per stretch, "451–455 MHz, 37 channels") rather than emitters, and a "new since baseline" filter.
  - A fast survey (200–605 MHz, a pass every 0.3 s, 12 kHz bins) makes ~40 records/s, 78% one-pass 8–11 dB noise blips. Those no longer confirm emitters; ~290–330 confirmed remain after 6 min, mostly steady weak activity (200–260 MHz) and land mobile.
  - Survey emitters now group by overlap, and unconfirmed blips (once, weak, brief) are hidden and expire. A full-band survey settles to ~220 confirmed emitters after 5 min, still adding ~0.2/s (new land-mobile activity, varying LTE/TV blobs). Humps of raised floor (e.g. 685–688 MHz) still split into ~300 kHz pieces.
- **Hop profile is estimated live** (median across hops, smoothed across passes). It removes passband ripple and fixed-offset spurs. Consider saving it per serial and gain so a fresh sweep starts clean.
- **Sweep is now one hop per USB transfer** on an Airspy (retune as a transfer arrives, 3 ms latency, R820T lock flag for slow VCO searches): about 2x the old 9.5 ms guard. What's left:
  - About 6 real VCO searches per full pass (~13 ms each, at the divider boundaries near 28, 55, 110, 218, 442 and 883 MHz). Hop centres could be placed so these land on the wrap-around, or a 4th FFT skipped there.
  - Busy hops whose carrier level moves between FFTs (LTE 700) used to trip the settle check; now only a first-FFT outlier counts. Watch for broadband stripes coming back.
  - The band-power probe (`slow_hops`) remains for devices without a lock flag; untested on anything but the Airspy.
  - HydraSDR RFOne should have the same R82x lock flag; check when one is on hand.
- **Survey waterfall fills slowly** (one row per pass). Consider updating the newest row hop by hop as the pass progresses.

## Detection

- **RDS (and HD Radio PSD) on detected FM broadcast stations.** When a wideband FM signal is identified, decode the 57 kHz RDS subcarrier automatically (PI, PS name, RadioText, PTY, AF) and label the emitter with the station name; HD Radio programme service data (title/artist) once HD decode exists. Needs a stereo-multiplex-capable demod path per station and a small RDS decoder (BPSK at 1187.5 bps, block sync, error correction).
- **ATSC / ATSC 3.0 programme data.** ATSC 1.0 is 8-VSB in 6 MHz, so an R2 at 10 MS/s sees a whole channel; channel names and the programme guide live in the PSIP tables (VCT, EIT, ETT) of the 19.4 Mb/s transport stream, which means a full 8-VSB receiver (timing, equaliser, trellis + RS decoding) before any table can be read: feasible in software (GNU Radio has one) but CPU-heavy and a big piece of work. ATSC 3.0 is OFDM with LDPC; the bootstrap and L1 signalling are open and would at least identify the service, but service lists (SLT over ROUTE/IP) need most of a 3.0 receiver, and no mature open demodulator exists.
- **LTE / NR cell identification (PSS/SSS/MIB).** The spectral identifier only infers LTE/NR from a flat block of a standard width inside a downlink allocation. A focus visit can prove it: cyclic-prefix autocorrelation first (lag 66.7 µs, period 71.4 µs at 15 kHz subcarrier spacing; half that for NR at 30 kHz; blind, no sync, also separates LTE/NR from ATSC 3.0, DVB-T, DAB and HD Radio by numerology), then the LTE primary/secondary sync sequences in the central 1.08 MHz every 5 ms (three Zadoff-Chu PSS roots, SSS) for the exact centre, frame timing and physical cell ID 0–503, and the MIB on PBCH (QPSK, rate-1/3 tail-biting convolutional code, 40 ms TTI) for the true bandwidth in resource blocks, system frame number and antenna count. NR uses an m-sequence PSS in a 240-subcarrier SSB on the GSCN raster, not necessarily at carrier centre, PCI 0–1007; correlating against both is how LTE and NR are told apart in the shared low bands. Runs on a 1.92 MS/s DDC, so even a 20 MHz carrier's sync block fits the R2's clean span. Verdict for auto-explore: "LTE 10 MHz PCI 217". Operator identity (PLMN) lives only in SIB1, which needs a PDCCH/PDSCH chain (turbo decoding, RRC ASN.1) and the full carrier in view; not planned. Operator comes from the band plan instead: band 13 Verizon and band 14 FirstNet nationwide, bands 5/12/17/71 per market from FCC licence data, so a per-market licensee table in `bandplan` closes most of the gap.
- **GSM detector.** GSM is shut down in the US (AT&T 2017, T-Mobile's last cells wound down through 2025) but common elsewhere, and a surviving beacon is unmistakable: a 200 kHz GMSK carrier at 270.833 kbaud whose BCCH is on continuously, with the frequency-correction burst (a pure tone 67.7 kHz above centre, 142 bits) every 10 frames as the cheap signature. Detect carrier width + FCCH tone, then the SCH training sequence for BSIC (network colour code and base-station colour code) as the identity. Small.
- **Auto-squelch flashes on wideband signals.** Wide signals (TV, LTE, FM broadcast) drift in and out of the spectral squelch between rows, so they flash. Needs hysteresis and a hold time per occupant, and an occupant extent that is the union over recent rows rather than the latest one.
- **HD Radio (NRSC-5) decode.** The hybrid digital sidebands are already detected in Focus; decoding them (OFDM sync, layer 1 FEC, HDC audio) is a large piece of work and the audio codec is proprietary, so start with station/programme metadata (SIS) and see how far the open descriptions go.
- **Decoding next steps.** P25 NID (NAC/DUID, BCH-corrected), DMR slot type (colour code/data type, Golay-corrected) and NXDN LICH are decoded and checked against real captures (`detect/tests/real_captures.rs`). Next: P25 TSBK (control channel messages: system/site IDs, grants → follow voice), DMR CSBK, DMR EMB (colour code on voice bursts), NXDN SACCH/RAN, P25 Phase 2. Also confirm the NXDN direction bit against a known transmitter.
- **Per-protocol re-slicing.** The prefilter cutoff is chosen before the protocol is known (4–6.5 kHz). Weak DMR improved further at 4 kHz (22 → ~50 slot types), so re-slice with a protocol-specific cutoff once identified.
- **Features are weak on real signals.** On hardware, FSK level counts and symbol rates often come out blank even where the sync bank confirms a protocol (NXDN48 at 460 MHz with ×8–18 syncs, DMR at 855.59 with ×15–33). Deviation also reads high (noise, discriminator clicks). Once a sync is found, use its timing to slice symbols properly (levels set per sync) and measure levels/deviation from that, instead of a raw histogram.
- **TDMA single-slot traffic fragments into many short records** (e.g. 47 records in 45 s at 856.3375), each too short to hold two syncs, so they stay unidentified. Group records by channel/emitter and classify the group, or lengthen the hangover for slotted signals.
- **Unverified labels:** ProVoice (binary sync, 2-bit tolerance) seen ×3 at 854.71 MHz, and the X2-TDMA patterns. Treat as provisional until checked against a known transmitter.

- **Spectral identifier / squelch, open items** (`detect::spectral`, `engine::occupancy`; regression in `detect/tests/real_spectra.rs`):
  - A faint flat TV channel without a pilot (ch 35, 596–602 MHz, +4.8 dB between two ATSC 1.0 neighbours; likely ATSC 3.0) isn't found: the flat-block test needs ≥ 3 dB with ≤ 3 dB ripple and an edge dip.
  - LTE needs ≥ 20 MHz in view (a Focus span can sit inside a carrier with no floor to judge by); Focus panes rely on the survey's list. Uplink bands aren't identified (bursty).
  - Survey FM detection at 54 kHz bins works; HD sidebands need ≤ 15 kHz bins (Focus).
  - Known occupants are kept in memory per source; persisting them per location would let a fresh start squelch at once.
- **Carriers vs spurs** (`detect::carrier`, `detect::context`): DC, the radio's 5 MHz spur grid, the USB-transfer tone on internal spurs (152.6 Hz at 10 MS/s), CW birdies (pure, steady lines) and CTCSS tones are labelled. An airband birdie comb at 200 kHz spacing (119.6, 119.8, 122.2–122.8 MHz) is sometimes still called "Aviation AM" when the line is weak; detecting the comb itself (equal spacing) would settle it.
- **ISM bands:** LoRa chirp detection (`detect::chirp`) is only tested on synthetic chirps: 433.92 MHz was silent for 90 s and nothing at 902–928 chirped. 902–928 here is ~155 ms narrow bursts hopping between 904.99, 905.79, 910.60, 914.80 and 915.80 MHz with ±25 kHz sidelines (likely FHSS meters); grouping hops across emitters would name them.
- **Talk activity:** DMR voice/control/data from slot types and voice syncs, talkgroup/source from voice LC headers (BPTC + RS(12,9)), CSBK opcodes (Cap+ system status confirmed by CRC on a real capture); P25 from DUIDs, with the talkgroup from control-channel grants matched by frequency; NXDN from the LICH. P25 voice link control (`detect::p25_lc`: TDULC Golay + RS, LDU1 Hamming + RS) names talkgroup and radio on traffic and conventional channels; checked against control-channel grants recorded at the same time (`testdata/captures/P25_site_*`). Vendor link control (MFID > 1, ~20% of words here) isn't parsed. P25 Phase 2 (`detect::p25p2`): superframe fragments, ISCH, DUID, descrambling (WACN/system from the Phase 1 control channel via the engine's monitor; NAC from Phase 1 NIDs or Phase 2 end-of-PTT), RS(63,35) with punctured symbols as erasures, MAC PTT / group / unit voice → talkgroup and radio. ~89% of scrambled FACCH decode on the local site's three TDMA channels (`testdata/captures/P25P2_site_*`). Without the control channel in view, scrambled bursts stay undecoded (only unscrambled end-of-PTT talkgroups). Other MAC opcodes (0x42, 0x7C, ...) aren't parsed. Next: DMR embedded LC on voice bursts (needs DMR voice to test on; none heard locally yet). Weak DMR slicing is much better since the per-sync slicer (41 of 58 CSBKs pass on the 855.59 capture, was 4 of 20).
- **Pagers** (`detect::flex`, `detect::pocsag`, pager monitors in `engine::monitor`, Pages tab):
  - FLEX 3200/4 decodes on real signal (929.6125 MHz). The 1600/2, 1600/4 and 3200/2 speeds are implemented from the spec but untested: nothing local sends them.

  - Decoded: talkgroup / radio / voice channel from Cap+ group voice link control; emitters learn their own repeater number from it ("CC 9 · Cap+ rep 3"). Untested on real voice: none heard yet.

## Recording / look-back

- **Longer look-back buffer.** It's 10 s per device now (`ring_seconds`), ci16 at 10 MS/s = 40 MB/s, so 30 s is 1.2 GB per device, not under a GB; packing the Airspy's 12-bit samples (3 bytes per complex sample) gets 30 s to 0.9 GB, and dropping the dirty edges (9 of the 10 MHz) only saves 10% and needs a resampler. Make it configurable (a couple of minutes = ~5 GB at ci16) and show the cost in the settings UI.
- **Record every signal in the watched range.** Besides "record the whole span", a mode that writes one channel capture per detected signal (DDC'd to its own bandwidth, start/stop with the record), named by frequency and label, so a busy range yields many small files instead of one huge one.

## Hardware

- **Manual Airspy gain stages** (LNA/Mixer/VGA individually). The UI has the linearity/sensitivity presets and AGC.
- **tinySA Ultra support** (on hand). A swept spectrum analyzer, not an SDR: no IQ, so no look-back or decoding. Three uses:
  - **Calibration reference:** reads in dBm, so measuring the same signal on both gives per-device dBFS→dBm offsets for the Airspys.
  - **Test source:** its signal-generator output gives known tones to measure Airspy gain tables and passband shape, and to test detection repeatably.
  - **Survey outside the R2's range:** about 100 kHz–6 GHz (HF below 24 MHz; 1.8–6 GHz for cellular, Wi-Fi, drone links).
  - Needs a second kind of source, a *power-sweep device* producing spectrum rows directly (USB serial text protocol). The Survey role and burst detector already work on rows. Check its COM port and firmware commands before writing it.
- **R2s hear each other's local oscillator.** Each R2 radiates a little LO energy out of its antenna port (strongest at the top edge of its span, centre + 5 MHz, plus weaker spurs); the other R2 picks it up even 10 ft away. Confirmed by swapping roles. The UI hides survey detections inside the Focus span (the focus is the better witness there). Better: model each device's known spur offsets and mask them wherever the *other* device sweeps, and suggest a few dB of attenuation or a filter on the survey antenna.
- **Two R2s on one USB bus interfere.** With both R2s sharing a hub, the sweeping one put a dense comb of narrow carriers into the other (856.6–859.5 MHz). On separate buses it's gone. Worth a note in docs and maybe a warning when two Airspys share a hub.
- **A second Airspy sometimes can't be opened while the first streams** (`AIRSPY_ERROR_NOT_FOUND`), seen in the `classify_sim` example but not in the app. libairspy opens each device in turn to read serials; the exact trigger isn't known yet.
- **Recover when a device is unplugged** (the `DeviceLost` event is defined but nothing reacts to it).
